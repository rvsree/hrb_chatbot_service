Policy Document for U.S. Employees
Plan Year: January 1, 2026 – December 31, 2026
​

The JPMorgan Chase Health Benefits Program provides comprehensive medical, dental, and vision coverage to eligible employees and their dependents. This document outlines the 2026 plan provisions, including coverage options, eligibility requirements, enrollment procedures, costs, wellness incentives, and continuation rights for both active and former employees.
​

JPMorgan Chase is committed to providing comprehensive, high-quality healthcare benefits that support the physical and financial well-being of employees and their families. The Health Benefits Program offers choice, flexibility, and value through a variety of plan options and wellness initiatives.
​

JPMorgan Chase Health Benefits Program – Executive Summary
The 2026 plan year runs from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026, with annual enrollment typically occurring in November for coverage starting January 1 of the following year.
​

JPMorgan Chase & Co. serves as Plan Sponsor and Administrator, with third-party vendors providing claims processing, customer service, and network management:
​

Medical plans: Aetna, Cigna Healthcare, UnitedHealthcare, Centivo (select markets)

Dental plans: MetLife, Delta Dental

Vision plan: VSP (Vision Service Plan)

Prescription drug coverage: CVS Caremark
​

Section 1: Program Overview and Philosophy
1.1 Purpose
The Health Benefits Program is designed to promote employee health and well-being while providing financial protection against healthcare costs.
​

1.2 Plan Year
Plan year: January 1, 2026 – December 31, 2026

Annual enrollment: Typically November, for coverage effective January 1
​

1.3 Program Administration
JPMorgan Chase & Co. is Plan Sponsor and Administrator and partners with national and regional health vendors for plan operations.
​

Section 2: Eligibility
2.1 Employee Eligibility
Employees are eligible for health benefits if they:
​

Are regularly scheduled to work 20 or more hours per week

Are on a U.S. payroll

Are not classified as temporary, seasonal, or contingent workers

Coverage effective date:

Coverage becomes effective on the first day of the month following or coinciding with the date of hire, if enrollment is completed within 30 days.
​

New hire enrollment period:

Newly eligible employees have 30 days from their hire date to enroll.

Failure to enroll results in waiver of coverage until the next annual enrollment or a qualifying life event (QLE).
​

2.2 Dependent Eligibility
Eligible dependents include:
​

Spouse: Legal spouse under applicable state or federal law

Domestic partner: Same-sex or opposite-sex domestic partner meeting firm eligibility rules

Children: Biological, legally adopted, placed for adoption, stepchildren, and children under legal guardianship

Dependent child age limits:

Children are eligible until the end of the calendar year in which they turn age 26.

No student status, marital status, or tax dependency requirements.

Disabled dependent children may be eligible beyond age 26 with proper documentation and approval.
​

2.3 Verification Requirements
JPMorgan Chase may require documentation to verify dependent eligibility, such as:
​

Marriage certificate

Birth certificate

Adoption decree

Domestic partnership affidavit

Court order establishing guardianship

Failure to provide requested documentation may result in termination of dependent coverage.
​

2.4 Former Employee Eligibility (Retirees)
Retiree health benefits:

Employees who retire from JPMorgan Chase may be eligible to continue health coverage if they meet pre-2012 eligibility criteria; the Retiree Medical Plan closed to new participants effective December 31, 2012.
​

Eligible retirees:

Hired before January 1, 2013

Met age and service requirements as of December 31, 2012

Retired directly from active employment (not after termination and rehire)
​

Employees hired on or after January 1, 2013 are not eligible for retiree medical benefits.

Be enrolled in an HSA-qualified HDHP

Not be covered by other non-HDHP health coverage

Not be enrolled in Medicare

Not be claimed as a dependent on another person’s tax return

Section 10: Benefits Continuation for Former Employees
10.1 COBRA Continuation Coverage
Under COBRA, employees and dependents may continue group health coverage after certain qualifying events.
​

Eligible individuals:

Employees who terminate (other than for gross misconduct) or have reduced hours

Spouses/partners losing coverage due to termination, reduction, divorce, or death

Dependent children losing eligibility
​

Maximum COBRA duration (typical):
​

Termination/reduced hours: 18 months

Divorce/legal separation: 36 months

Death of employee: 36 months

Dependent losing eligibility: 36 months

Medicare entitlement affecting dependents: 36 months

Disability extension:

Up to 29 months total if SSA determines disability within first 60 days of COBRA coverage.
​

COBRA cost:

Participants pay 102% of full premium (employee + employer share plus 2% admin fee).
​

COBRA enrollment process:

Receive election notice within 14 days.

Return election form within 60 days.

Pay first premium within 45 days (coverage retroactive).

Pay ongoing monthly premiums.
​

COBRA ends when:

Maximum period ends

Premiums not paid

Individual gains other group coverage

Individual becomes Medicare-eligible

Employer ceases to offer any group plan
​

10.2 Retiree Medical Coverage
As noted, retiree medical is available only to certain grandfathered employees.
​

Options may include:

JPMorgan Chase-sponsored retiree plans

Medicare Advantage or supplement plans for age 65+
​

Retirees pay age-, tier-, and plan-based premiums; some may receive subsidies based on service and retirement date.
​

Medicare coordination:

Medicare Parts A and B become primary at 65; JPMC retiree coverage is secondary.
​

10.3 Extension of Benefits Upon Termination
Coverage generally continues through the end of the month of termination if premiums are paid.

HSA funds remain with former employees and can be used for qualified expenses indefinitely, but new contributions require HDHP coverage elsewhere.
​

11.3 Claims Appeals Process
If claims are denied or benefits are lower than expected, members may appeal.
​

Level 1 appeal:

Submit written appeal with supporting documentation within 180 days.

Carrier decides within about 30 days (medical) or 60 days (disability).
​

Level 2 appeal:

Request second-level review within 60 days of Level 1 denial.

Independent review with decision typically within 30–60 days.
​

External review:

Available for certain denials (e.g., medical necessity, experimental treatments) after exhausting internal appeals.
​

Expedited appeals:

For urgent care where standard timeframes could jeopardize health, decisions are made within about 72 hours.
​
