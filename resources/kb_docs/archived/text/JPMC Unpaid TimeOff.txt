Policy Document for U.S. Employees
Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) | Personal Leave (Unpaid)
Effective Date: January 1, 2025
​

JPMorgan Chase provides unpaid leave benefits under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) and the firm’s Personal Leave policy. This document outlines FMLA leave (up to 12 weeks for qualifying family/medical reasons, or 26 weeks for military caregiver leave) and Personal Leave (up to 26 weeks for personal reasons not covered by other policies).
​

These policies provide job-protected time off while maintaining employment continuity and benefit eligibility.
​

JPMorgan Chase Unpaid Leave Policies – Executive Summary
The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) is a federal law providing eligible employees with up to 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12‑month period for specified family and medical reasons, and up to 26 workweeks for military caregiver leave.
​

JPMorgan Chase complies with FMLA and often runs paid leave (Parental Leave, Critical Caregiver Leave, Short‑Term Disability) concurrently with FMLA.
​

Section 1: Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)
1.1 FMLA Overview
Key FMLA features:
​

Up to 12 workweeks of unpaid leave per 12‑month period (26 weeks for military caregiver)

Job protection with return to same or equivalent position

Continuation of group health benefits during leave

Protection from retaliation for taking FMLA leave

Federal law applicable to covered employers

JPMorgan Chase administers FMLA in full compliance with regulations and coordinates FMLA with its paid leave policies.
​

1.2 FMLA Eligibility Requirements
To qualify, employees must:
​

Have worked for JPMorgan Chase for at least 12 months (not necessarily consecutive)

Have at least 1,250 hours worked in the 12 months immediately before leave

Work at a location with at least 50 employees within 75 miles

Additional notes:

Prior JPMC service may count toward the 12 months.

1,250 hours ≈ 24 hours/week over 12 months, so some part‑time employees qualify.

Employees not eligible for FMLA may still qualify for other JPMC leaves.
​

1.3 Qualifying Reasons for FMLA Leave
FMLA leave may be taken for:
​

Birth and care of newborn

Birth of employee’s child and bonding in the first 12 months; available to all parents.

Adoption or foster placement

Placement for adoption or foster care and bonding within 12 months.

Care for family member with serious health condition

Spouse, domestic partner, child (under 18 or incapable of self‑care due to disability), or parent.

Employee’s own serious health condition

Illness/injury making the employee unable to perform essential job functions, including pregnancy and chronic conditions.

Qualifying exigency related to military service

For spouse, child, or parent on covered active duty or call to active duty (e.g., deployment, military events, childcare, financial/legal arrangements, counseling, rest/recuperation, post‑deployment).

Military caregiver leave (26 weeks)

Care for covered service member (spouse, child, parent, or next of kin) with serious injury/illness incurred in line of duty; up to 26 workweeks in a single 12‑month period.
​

1.4 Serious Health Condition Defined
A “serious health condition” includes:
​

Inpatient care:

Overnight stay in hospital, hospice, or residential medical facility plus related treatment.

Continuing treatment:

More than 3 consecutive days of incapacity plus treatment by a healthcare provider.

Pregnancy/prenatal care.

Chronic serious conditions (e.g., asthma, diabetes, epilepsy) requiring periodic provider visits.

Permanent/long‑term incapacity (e.g., Alzheimer’s, stroke, terminal illness).

Conditions requiring multiple treatments (e.g., chemotherapy, dialysis, major surgery).

Examples:

Cancer and treatment

Heart attack, stroke, serious heart conditions

Major surgery and recovery

Pregnancy complications

Mental health conditions needing hospitalization or ongoing treatment

Chronic back or respiratory conditions
​

Generally not serious health conditions:

Common colds/flu without complications

Routine exams

Cosmetic treatments unless medically necessary

Minor injuries not requiring continuing treatment
​

1.5 FMLA Leave Duration and Calculation
Standard FMLA leave:
​

Up to 12 workweeks of unpaid leave per 12‑month period.

One workweek equals the number of days the employee normally works.

Military caregiver leave:

Up to 26 workweeks of unpaid leave in a single 12‑month period.

Combined total FMLA (standard + military caregiver) in that period cannot exceed 26 weeks.
​

12‑month period:

JPMC uses a rolling 12‑month period measured backward from the date FMLA leave is used.

Remaining entitlement is 12 weeks minus FMLA used in the preceding 12 months.
​

Types of FMLA leave:
​

Continuous: Consecutive days/weeks (e.g., surgery recovery, childbirth).

Intermittent: Separate blocks of time for a single qualifying reason (e.g., chemo, flare‑ups), when medically necessary; not available for bonding leave.

Reduced schedule: Reduced work schedule when medically necessary (e.g., post‑surgery).

1.6 Intermittent/Reduced Leave and Notice
Intermittent/reduced schedule rules:
​

JPMC may temporarily transfer employees to an alternative equivalent position to accommodate schedules.

FMLA time is tracked in the smallest increment used in payroll (typically 1 hour).

Employee responsibilities:

Make reasonable efforts to schedule foreseeable treatment outside work hours.

Provide advance notice of appointments.

Follow call‑in procedures for unscheduled absences.
​

1.7 FMLA Notice Requirements
Foreseeable leave (planned):

Provide at least 30 days’ notice when possible.

If 30 days not possible, give notice as soon as practicable (1–2 business days after knowing of need).

Notify supervisor and contact Benefits Service Center (1‑844‑ASK‑JPMC).

Complete leave request forms and provide medical certification.
​

Unforeseeable leave (emergent):

Notify supervisor as soon as practicable (same day or next business day).

Follow normal call‑in procedures.

Contact Benefits Service Center within 2 business days and provide certification within 15 days, if required.
​

Failure to provide notice may delay or result in denial of FMLA protection absent good cause.
​

1.8 Medical Certification Requirements
Certification is required for:
​

Employee’s own serious health condition

Family member’s serious health condition

Serious injury/illness of covered service member

Certification must include:

Condition start date and expected duration

Diagnosis and prognosis

Medical facts

Statement that employee is needed to provide care (for family) or cannot work (for self)

Estimated time needed and, for intermittent/reduced leave, schedule details
​

Timing:

Employee has 15 days from request to provide certification; extensions may be granted.

Certification is submitted to the Benefits Service Center, not the supervisor, and kept confidential.
​

If certification is incomplete:

JPMC will notify the employee of deficiencies and allow 7 days to cure; failure may result in denial.
​

Recertification:

Usually no more often than every 30 days unless circumstances change or doubt arises.

For chronic conditions, not more than every 6 months in most cases.
​

Second/third opinions:

JPMC may request second and, if needed, third (binding) opinions at employer expense if questions arise about the initial certification.
​

1.9 Job Protection and Restoration Rights
Upon return from FMLA, employees are entitled to:
​

Same position or an equivalent position with equivalent pay, benefits, and terms.

No loss of accrued benefits and continued seniority/service credit.

No adverse action based on taking FMLA leave.

Equivalent position:

Same or virtually identical pay (including differentials and incentives).

Same or equivalent benefits.

Similar duties, responsibilities, and authority.

Similar schedule and location.
​

Key employee exception:

In rare cases, job restoration may be denied to “key employees” (top 10% highly paid salaried employees within 75 miles) if restoration would cause substantial economic injury; JPMC must notify the employee of this status and opportunity to return.
​

Restoration not required if:

Employee would have been terminated or laid off regardless of FMLA (e.g., RIF).

FMLA was obtained fraudulently.

Employee fails to return at the end of leave or indicates intent not to return.
​

1.10 Benefits Continuation During FMLA Leave
Group health benefits (medical, dental, vision) continue at the same level and cost as before leave.
​

Premium payment:
​

Paid leave: Employee premiums deducted from pay as usual.

Unpaid leave: Employee is billed directly and must pay within required time (typically 30 days; 15‑day grace notice) or coverage may be suspended.

Upon return, coverage is reinstated with no waiting periods or pre‑existing condition exclusions.
​

Other benefits:

Life and accident insurance, disability coverage, and 401(k) status may continue or be affected per plan rules; accruals may be suspended during unpaid leave, but seniority/service credit continues.
​

If employee does not return after FMLA for reasons other than continued serious health condition or circumstances beyond control, JPMC may seek reimbursement of health premiums paid during unpaid FMLA.
​

1.11 Interaction with JPMC Paid Leave Policies
FMLA runs concurrently with JPMC paid leaves when applicable.
​

Examples:

Parental Leave: 16 weeks paid; first 12 weeks satisfy FMLA (if eligible); remaining 4 weeks are protected under JPMC policy.

Critical Caregiver Leave: 4 weeks paid; runs concurrently with FMLA when caring for a spouse/domestic partner/child/parent with a serious condition; remaining FMLA up to 12 weeks is unpaid.

Short‑Term Disability: Up to 26 weeks of paid benefits; runs concurrently with FMLA for qualifying serious health conditions.

Sick time: Can be used as pay while FMLA provides job protection, and runs concurrently when used for FMLA‑qualifying reasons.
​

State paid family leave:

Typically runs concurrently with FMLA; FMLA provides job protection while state benefits provide wage replacement; JPMC paid leave may supplement state benefits.
​

1.12 Intermittent Leave Administration
Tracking:

Leave is tracked in the smallest increment used in payroll (often 1 hour); each hour counts against the 12‑week entitlement based on normal weekly hours.
​

Employer rights:

JPMC may temporarily reassign employees to equivalent roles that better accommodate intermittent or reduced schedules.

Reasonable scheduling procedures and status reporting may be required.
​

1.13 Returning from FMLA Leave
Fitness‑for‑duty:

For leave based on the employee’s own serious health condition, JPMC may require fitness‑for‑duty certification before return.

Certification may be required for each intermittent absence if justified by safety concerns and consistent with prior notice.
​

Return process:
​

Confirm return date with supervisor/HR.

Provide fitness‑for‑duty certification if required.

Complete any required forms.

Resume same or equivalent position.

Failure to return:

May be treated as voluntary resignation if employee does not return or request an extension; return may be delayed for insufficient certification.
​

Extensions:

If additional time is needed, employees may request Personal Leave (up to 26 weeks total) before FMLA ends; job protection beyond FMLA is limited.
​

1.14 FMLA Protections and Non‑Retaliation
Employees exercising FMLA rights are protected from:
​

Termination or discipline for taking FMLA leave

Counting FMLA absences under “no‑fault” attendance policies

Loss of accrued benefits

Retaliation or interference with FMLA rights

JPMC will not:
​

Discourage the use of FMLA

Manipulate hours to avoid eligibility

Use FMLA leave as a negative factor in employment decisions

Retaliate for filing FMLA complaints

Recourse:

Internal complaints to HR/Employee Relations

DOL Wage and Hour Division complaints

Private lawsuits (subject to statutory deadlines)
​

1.15 FMLA Administration and Contact
Leave request process:
​

Notify supervisor of need for leave.

Contact Benefits Service Center (1‑844‑ASK‑JPMC).

Complete FMLA request forms and medical certification.

Submit documentation to leave administrator.

Receive FMLA designation notice (approved/denied).

Coordinate leave details with supervisor/HR.

JPMC provides required notices (general, eligibility, rights/responsibilities, designation) and employees should retain copies of all leave documentation.
​

Section 2: Personal Leave (Unpaid)
2.1 Personal Leave Overview
Personal Leave is an unpaid leave option for extended absences not covered by FMLA, STD, or other policies.
​

Key features:

Up to 26 weeks of unpaid leave in a rolling 12‑month period

Minimum 4 weeks of job‑protected leave

For personal reasons (extended matters, non‑FMLA family obligations, education, relocation, other personal needs)

Approval is discretionary based on business needs

Benefits continuation available with employee premium payments
​

2.2 Eligibility Requirements
To request Personal Leave, employees must:
​

Be actively employed on U.S. payroll

Be benefits‑eligible (20+ hours/week)

Have a satisfactory performance record

Not be under active disciplinary action

No minimum service or hours requirement; all benefits‑eligible employees may request Personal Leave.
​

Approval is discretionary and depends on business needs, HR and manager review, and the nature/duration of the requested leave.
​

2.3 Personal Leave Duration
Up to 26 weeks unpaid leave in a rolling 12‑month period (measured backward from start of leave).

First 4 weeks are job‑protected (right to same or equivalent position).

After 4 weeks, job protection is not guaranteed, though reasonable efforts are made to reinstate.
​

Extensions:

May be requested before current leave expires; subject to manager and HR approval and not guaranteed.
​

2.4 Requesting Personal Leave
Notice:

Recommended: 30 days’ notice for foreseeable leave; minimum 2 weeks when possible.

Emergencies: Notify as soon as practicable.
​

Process:

Discuss with supervisor.

Contact HR or Benefits Service Center (1‑844‑ASK‑JPMC).

Complete Personal Leave request form.

Provide supporting documentation if requested.

Await decision (typically within 5–10 business days).
​

Required information:

General reason for leave, start date, duration, expected return date, and contact information while on leave.
​

Supporting documentation may include medical notes, enrollment verification for education, or other appropriate verification.
​

Approval criteria:

Business impact, coverage options, duration, performance history, prior leave frequency, and department needs.
​

Denial possible when business needs cannot accommodate absence, during critical periods, or when performance/attendance concerns exist.
​

2.5 Benefits Continuation During Personal Leave
Health benefits:

Employees may continue medical/dental/vision coverage at the full premium cost (employee + employer share), paid after‑tax via direct billing.
​

Premiums:

Invoices sent to home; first invoice typically due within 45 days, subsequent within 30 days; failure to pay results in termination of coverage.

Medical coverage may be reinstated within 6 months by paying past‑due premiums.
​

Life and accident insurance:

Basic life continues at no cost during approved leave.

Supplemental life and AD&D may continue via direct bill; business travel accident coverage is suspended.
​

Disability coverage:

STD does not apply during unpaid Personal Leave.

LTD may continue with premium payments.
​

Spending accounts/401(k):

Health Care FSA may continue with after‑tax contributions; stopping contributions ends participation.

Dependent Care FSA is suspended; only claims incurred before leave are reimbursable.

Transportation accounts are terminated; unused credits may be forfeited.

401(k) contributions and employer match are suspended during unpaid leave; service/vesting may continue up to certain limits, and contributions resume upon return.
​

Vacation and sick time:

Accruals are suspended during unpaid Personal Leave; existing balances remain and accrual resumes upon return.
​

Other voluntary benefits (e.g., legal, excess liability) may continue via premiums; EAP remains available.
​

2.6 Employment Status During Personal Leave
Job protection:

First 4 weeks: Return to same or equivalent position is guaranteed.

After 4 weeks: Reinstatement is not guaranteed but reasonable efforts are made to place the employee in a comparable position.
​

Employment continuity:

Employment continues during approved leave; service credit may accrue up to certain limits and policies continue to apply.

Employees must maintain agreed communication with supervisor/HR and provide updates or extension requests as needed.
​

2.7 Returning from Personal Leave
Return process:

Contact supervisor/HR at least 2 weeks before planned return.

Confirm return date.

Complete any required documentation.

Attend return‑to‑work meetings if requested.

Resume duties in assigned position.
​

Reinstatement:

Within 4 weeks: Return to same or equivalent role is guaranteed.

After 4 weeks: Reinstatement to a comparable role is based on availability, with no guarantee of same team, location, or supervisor.
​

Early return:

May be requested and approved based on business needs; 2 weeks’ notice is recommended.
​

Failure to return:

If employee does not return as scheduled and does not communicate, JPMC may treat it as voluntary resignation, end benefits coverage, and process final pay per policy/state law.
​

2.8 Personal Leave vs Other Leave Options
Personal Leave is appropriate when:

Extended time off is needed for reasons not covered by FMLA, STD, Parental Leave, Critical Caregiver Leave, or military leave, and vacation/personal days are insufficient.
​

Employees should consider:

FMLA for qualifying serious health or family reasons (if eligible).

Parental Leave for birth/adoption.

Critical Caregiver Leave for serious health events of close family.

STD for employee illness/injury.

Military leave for service obligations.

Vacation/personal days for shorter absences.
​

Coordination with FMLA:

Personal Leave may run concurrently with FMLA when FMLA‑eligible and the reason qualifies.

FMLA provides 12 weeks of job protection; Personal Leave may extend total unpaid time to 26 weeks, but job protection after FMLA is limited to 4 weeks under Personal Leave.
​

2.9 Termination of Personal Leave
Personal Leave ends when:
​

Approved period expires and employee returns.

Employee resigns or obtains other employment.

Employee fails to return without communication.

Maximum 26 weeks is reached.

Employee fails to maintain required premium payments.

If extension is denied, employee must return on the original end date or employment may be terminated.
​

Upon termination:

Health coverage ends at the end of the month, with COBRA available for medical/dental/vision.

Final paycheck and unused vacation payout follow policy/state law.

401(k) remains in the plan or may be rolled over.
​

3.2 Manager and HR Responsibilities
Managers:

Respond promptly and supportively to leave requests.

Maintain confidentiality.

Coordinate with HR on approvals and transitions.

Ensure no retaliation or adverse action for leave use.

Support reintegration on return.
​

HR:

Determine FMLA eligibility and designate leave.

Process and track leave requests.

Provide required notices.

Coordinate benefits and billing.

Ensure compliance with federal, state, and local laws.
​

3.3 State and Local Law Compliance
Many states/localities have leave laws that provide additional protections or pay (e.g., CA CFRA/PDL/PFL, NY PFL, NJ FLA/FLI, WA PFML, CT, MA, OR, RI, CO, DE, MD).
​

When FMLA, state law, and JPMC policy differ, the most generous provision for the employee applies.
​

Employees should contact HR or the Benefits Service Center for state‑specific guidance.
​

3.4 Privacy, Confidentiality, and Fraud Prevention
Confidentiality:

All leave‑related information (medical details, reasons for leave, provider communications) is stored securely and only accessible to authorized HR/leave administrators; supervisors receive only necessary information.
​

Compliance:

HIPAA protections apply to medical information, and ADA rules govern disability‑related inquiries and accommodations.
​

Good faith use:

Employees must use leave only for qualifying purposes, provide accurate information, and submit genuine documentation.
​

Misuse may result in denial, recovery of benefits, disciplinary action up to termination, loss of FMLA protections, and possible legal action.
​

Examples of fraud:

Working another job while on leave, falsifying medical forms, misrepresenting reasons for leave.
​
