Policy Document for U.S. Employees
Retirement Savings | Employer Contributions | Investment Options
Effective Date: January 1, 2025

The JPMorgan Chase 401(k) Savings Plan provides a comprehensive retirement savings program designed to help employees build financial security for their future. This policy document outlines the firm's 401(k) plan effective January 1, 2025, including automatic enrollment at 5% contribution rate with annual auto-escalation up to 10%, employer matching contributions of up to 5% (dollar-for-dollar) after one year of service, automatic pay credits of 3% to 5% for eligible employees earning less than $350,000 annually, immediate eligibility to participate with no waiting period, diverse investment options including Target Date Funds and professionally managed portfolios, 2025 contribution limits, vesting schedules for employer contributions, and loan and hardship withdrawal provisions.
​

The plan is administered through Empower Retirement with access via the mySedgwick portal, reflecting JPMorgan Chase's commitment to supporting employees' long-term financial wellness.
​

JPMorgan Chase 401(k) Savings Plan – Executive Summary
The JPMorgan Chase 401(k) Savings Plan is a tax-advantaged retirement savings program that enables employees to save and invest for their future while receiving valuable employer contributions.
​

Key plan features include:

Immediate eligibility to participate upon hire

Automatic enrollment at 5% contribution rate

Annual contribution auto-escalation (increases 1% per year up to 10%)

Employer matching contributions up to 5% (after one year of service)

Automatic pay credits of 3% to 5% for eligible employees

Tax-deferred and Roth (after-tax) contribution options

Wide range of investment choices including Target Date Funds

Loan provisions and hardship withdrawal options

Administered by Empower Retirement

Accessed through mySedgwick portal at login.mysedgwick.com/jpmc

Section 1: Plan Overview and Eligibility
1.1 Introduction to the 401(k) Savings Plan
The Plan is designed to help employees build retirement savings through a combination of employee contributions, employer matching contributions, and automatic pay credits.
​

1.2 Employee Eligibility
All employees are eligible to participate in the 401(k) Savings Plan if they:
​

Are employed by JPMorgan Chase or one of its subsidiaries that has adopted the Plan

Work in the United States

Are on a U.S. payroll

Additional eligibility details:

Employees are eligible to participate immediately upon hire.

There is no minimum service requirement to begin contributing.

Benefits-eligible employees (regularly scheduled to work 20+ hours per week) qualify for all plan features.

Part-time employees regularly scheduled to work 20 or more hours per week are eligible to participate on the same basis as full-time employees.
​

1.3 Automatic Enrollment
New hires are automatically enrolled in the Plan unless they opt out.
​

Automatic enrollment details:

Automatic contribution rate: 5% of Ongoing Compensation (before-tax)

Automatic investment election: Target Date Fund based on current age and assumed retirement age of 65

Contributions begin with the first paycheck after the grace period ends

Employees may decline participation during the grace period

Employees can opt out anytime after automatic enrollment

Employees can re-enroll at any time

Changes are made through the mySedgwick portal
​

Grace Period:

New hires have a 31-day grace period from their hire date before automatic enrollment takes effect.
​

If you do not actively decline participation or make contribution elections within the 31-day grace period:

You will be automatically enrolled in the Plan.

Contributions will be taken at the 5% default rate and invested in the default Target Date Fund.
​

Example (Automatic Enrollment Timing):

Employee hired April 1, 2025

April 1–May 1: 31-day grace period (no contributions unless employee elects)

May 2 onward: If no action taken, automatic 5% contributions begin
​

1.4 Annual Auto-Escalation
To help employees increase their retirement savings over time, the Plan includes an automatic escalation feature.
​

Automatic contribution increases:

Contribution rate automatically increases by 1% each year

Increases occur annually (typically in January)

Escalation continues until contribution rate reaches 10%

Applies to before-tax contribution rate

Employees can opt out or adjust the escalation schedule anytime

Employees can turn off automatic escalation, set a different target percentage, or change the annual increase amount via the mySedgwick portal
​

Example (Auto-Escalation):

Employee automatically enrolled at 5% in 2025:

January 2026: Contribution rate increases to 6%

January 2027: Contribution rate increases to 7%

January 2028: Contribution rate increases to 8%

Continues through January 2030 when reaching 10%
​

Section 2: Employee Contributions
2.1 Types of Contributions
Employees can make three types of contributions to the 401(k) Savings Plan:
​

Before-Tax (Traditional) Contributions:

Contributions made on a pre-tax basis (reduce current taxable income)

Taxes deferred until withdrawal in retirement

Earnings grow tax-deferred

Distributions subject to income tax upon withdrawal
​

Roth 401(k) Contributions:

Contributions made on an after-tax basis (no current tax deduction)

Qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free

Earnings grow tax-free

Must meet qualified distribution requirements: age 59½ and 5-year holding period
​

After-Tax (Non-Roth) Contributions:

Additional contributions beyond the annual limit for before-tax and Roth

Made on an after-tax basis

Earnings are tax-deferred (not tax-free)

Principal withdrawn tax-free; earnings taxed as ordinary income upon withdrawal
​

2.2 Contribution Limits for 2025
Annual contribution limits (Before-Tax + Roth combined) by age:
​

Under age 50: $23,500

Age 50–59: $31,000 ($23,500 + $7,500 catch-up)

Age 60–63: $34,750 ($23,500 + $11,250 enhanced catch-up)

Age 64 and older: $31,000 ($23,500 + $7,500 catch-up)
​

Enhanced Catch-Up Contributions (Ages 60–63):

Beginning in 2025, employees aged 60–63 can contribute an enhanced catch-up amount of $11,250 (total contribution up to $34,750).
​

After-Tax Contribution Limit:

Employees can contribute up to $10,000 annually in non-Roth after-tax contributions.
​

Overall Plan Limit:

Total annual additions (employee contributions + employer contributions) for 2025 is $69,000 (or $76,500 for employees age 50+).
​

Section 3: Employer Contributions
3.1 Matching Contributions
Employer match overview:
​

JPMorgan Chase provides dollar-for-dollar matching contributions on employee before-tax and/or Roth contributions up to 5% of Eligible Compensation.

Eligibility for matching contributions:

Employees must:

Complete one year of service (12 months from hire date)

Contribute to the Plan (before-tax and/or Roth)

Be actively employed on December 31 of the plan year

Have Total Annual Cash Compensation less than $1,000,000
​

Match calculation examples:

Employee earning $100,000 who contributes 5% ($5,000) receives a $5,000 match.

Employee earning $100,000 who contributes 3% ($3,000) receives a $3,000 match.
​

Matching contribution caps by compensation level:
​

Less than $350,000: Up to 5% of Eligible Compensation (no cap)

$350,000 to $999,999: Up to 5% of Eligible Compensation, capped at $10,000 annually

$1,000,000 or more: Not eligible for matching contributions

When matching contributions are made:

Matching contributions are made annually and credited in early the following year (e.g., 2025 match credited in early 2026).

Must be actively employed on December 31 to receive the match; termination before that date results in forfeiture of that year’s match.
​

Special consideration for new hires:

Only contributions made after completing one year of service are eligible for matching.
​

3.2 Automatic Pay Credits
Automatic pay credit overview:

In addition to matching contributions, JPMorgan Chase provides automatic pay credits to eligible employees.
​

Eligibility:

Employees must:

Complete one year of service

Have Total Annual Cash Compensation less than $350,000

Be actively employed on December 31 of the plan year
​

Automatic pay credit amounts:

For most employees, automatic pay credits equal 3% of Eligible Compensation, capped at $100,000 annually.
​

Enhanced automatic pay credits (grandfathered employees):

For benefits-eligible employees as of December 31, 2018, who are continuously employed, automatic pay credits range from 3% to 5% of Eligible Compensation (capped at $100,000) based on years of pay credit service.
​

Examples:

Employee earning $80,000 with one year of service: 3% × $80,000 = $2,400 automatic pay credit.

Employee earning $150,000 with one year of service: 3% × $100,000 (cap) = $3,000 automatic pay credit.
​

When automatic pay credits are made:

Credited annually and deposited to employee accounts in early the following year.

Must be actively employed on December 31 to receive credits.

Only compensation earned after completing one year of service is used to calculate automatic pay credits.
​

3.3 Maximizing Employer Contributions
Best practices:
​

Contribute at least 5% of Eligible Compensation to receive the full match (if eligible).

Contribute throughout the year so contributions are spread across all paychecks.

Remain employed through December 31 to receive annual employer contributions.

Monitor total compensation to understand how your compensation level affects match caps and pay credit eligibility.

Contribution pacing:

As long as total contributions reach 5% of annual Eligible Compensation by year-end, full match is received, regardless of contribution timing during the year.
​

Section 4: Vesting
4.1 What Is Vesting?
Vesting is the process by which employees earn non-forfeitable ownership rights to employer contributions.
​

Always 100% vested:

Employee contributions (before-tax, Roth, after-tax)

Rollover contributions from prior employer plans or IRAs

Investment earnings on employee contributions
​

Subject to vesting schedule (in general plans):

Employer matching contributions

Automatic pay credits

Investment earnings on employer contributions
​

4.2 JPMorgan Chase Vesting Schedule
For the JPMorgan Chase 401(k) Savings Plan, employer contributions (matching and automatic pay credits) are 100% immediately vested.
​

There is no waiting period to own employer contributions.

Employees retain 100% of employer contributions regardless of service length at termination.

No forfeiture of employer contributions upon separation.

All account balances are fully portable.
​

Vesting upon termination:

Because vesting is immediate, employees keep employer contributions when they leave.
​

5.2 Target Date Funds (Default Investment)
Target Date Funds are lifecycle investment options designed by JPMorgan Investment Management that automatically adjust asset allocation based on target retirement date.
​

Key features:

Professionally managed by Multi-Asset Solutions (MAS) within JPMorgan Investment Management

Diversified portfolios across equity, fixed income, REITs, inflation-protected securities, and cash

Automatic rebalancing over time to more conservative allocations as retirement approaches

Single-fund solution (no need to select multiple funds)

Default investment for automatically enrolled participants
​

How Target Date Funds work:

Funds with dates farthest in future have more aggressive allocations (higher equity exposure).

As target date approaches, asset mix gradually shifts to more conservative investments (bonds, cash).

After reaching target retirement date, the fund transitions to a Target Date Income Fund for retirees.
​

Available Target Date Funds:

Target Date 2025 Fund

Target Date 2030 Fund

Target Date 2035 Fund

Target Date 2040 Fund

Target Date 2045 Fund

Target Date 2050 Fund

Target Date 2055 Fund

Target Date 2060 Fund

Target Date 2065 Fund

Target Date Income Fund (for retirees)
​

Choosing a Target Date Fund:

Select the fund with a target date closest to the year you plan to retire.
​

Example:

Age 35 planning to retire at 65 (around 2055): Target Date 2055 Fund.

Age 50 planning to retire at 67 (around 2042): Target Date 2040 Fund.
​

5.3 Core Investment Funds

Diversification considerations:

Diversify across asset classes to manage risk.

Consider age and years to retirement when selecting allocation.

Rebalance periodically to maintain target allocation.

Section 6: Loans and Withdrawals
6.1 Plan Loans
The Plan permits participants to borrow from their vested account balance.
​

Loan terms:

Maximum loan amount: Lesser of $50,000 or 50% of vested account balance

Minimum loan amount: Typically $1,000 (check plan specifics)

Repayment term: Generally up to 5 years (longer for primary residence purchase)

Interest rate: Prime rate plus 1% (interest paid back to participant's own account)

Repayment method: Automatic payroll deduction
​

Loan advantages:

No credit check required

Interest paid to your own account

Loan does not appear on credit report

Generally lower interest rate than consumer loans
​

Loan disadvantages:

Reduces retirement account balance and potential investment growth

Repaid with after-tax dollars when contributions were pre-tax

If employment terminates, outstanding loan balance typically due within 60–90 days

Default on loan results in taxable income plus 10% penalty if under age 59½
​

How to request a loan:

Access mySedgwick portal.

Navigate to 401(k) Savings Plan.

Select “Request a Loan”.

Complete loan application specifying amount and purpose.

Review loan terms and repayment schedule.

Submit request.

Loan typically processed within 5–7 business days.
​

6.2 In-Service Withdrawals
Age 59½ withdrawals:

Once participants reach age 59½, they may take withdrawals while still employed.
​

Tax treatment:

No penalty for withdrawals after age 59½.

Ordinary income tax applies to pre-tax contributions and earnings.

Roth contributions and qualified earnings withdrawn tax-free.

No requirement to take withdrawals; participants may continue contributing and allow funds to grow.
​

After-tax contribution withdrawals:

Participants may withdraw after-tax (non-Roth) contributions at any time.

Principal withdrawn tax-free.

Earnings subject to ordinary income tax.

If under age 59½, a 10% penalty applies to earnings portion.
​

6.3 Hardship Withdrawals
The Plan permits hardship withdrawals for immediate and heavy financial needs.
​

Qualifying hardship reasons include:

Medical expenses for employee, spouse, or dependents

Costs related to purchase of primary residence (excluding mortgage payments)

Tuition and related educational fees for next 12 months for employee, spouse, or dependents

Payments to prevent eviction or foreclosure on primary residence

Funeral or burial expenses for certain family members

Expenses to repair damage to primary residence due to casualty

Expenses and losses due to federally declared disaster

Certain expenses related to birth or adoption of a child
​

Hardship withdrawal requirements:

Must demonstrate immediate and heavy financial need.

Withdrawal limited to amount necessary to satisfy need.

Must provide supporting documentation (e.g., medical bills, tuition statements, foreclosure notice).

Must have exhausted other available financial resources (including plan loans if available).
​

Tax consequences:

Hardship withdrawals subject to ordinary income tax.

If under age 59½, 10% early withdrawal penalty applies (some exceptions).

Hardship withdrawals cannot be repaid to the Plan.
​

How to request hardship withdrawal:

Access mySedgwick portal.

Navigate to “Request a Hardship Withdrawal”.

Complete application and specify reason and amount needed.

Upload supporting documentation.

Submit request.

Empower reviews application and documentation.

Approval decision typically within 7–10 business days.
​

6.4 Distributions Upon Termination
When employment ends, participants have several options for their 401(k) account balance:
​

Option 1: Leave Balance in Plan

If vested balance exceeds $7,000, can leave funds in the Plan.

Continue to manage investments and benefit from plan options.

No contributions allowed after termination and no further employer contributions.

Subject to plan fees and rules.
​

Option 2: Rollover to New Employer’s Plan

Transfer vested balance to new employer’s 401(k) or similar plan.

Avoid taxes and penalties if done as a direct rollover.

Consolidates retirement savings.

Subject to new plan’s rules and investment options.
​

Option 3: Rollover to IRA

Transfer vested balance to Traditional IRA or Roth IRA.

Broader investment choices than employer plans.

Avoids taxes and penalties if done as a direct rollover (taxes may apply for Traditional-to-Roth conversions).

May have different fee structures.
​

Option 4: Cash Distribution

Receive vested balance as lump-sum cash payment.

Subject to mandatory 20% federal tax withholding.

Ordinary income tax applies.

If under age 59½, 10% early withdrawal penalty applies.

Reduces retirement savings; generally not recommended unless immediate financial need.
​

Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs):

Upon reaching age 73 (for those born 1951 or later), participants must begin taking annual RMDs from the Plan if no longer employed by JPMorgan Chase.
​

Section 10: Important Legal and Tax Information
10.1 Tax Treatment of Contributions and Distributions
Before-tax contributions:

Reduce current taxable income.

Contributions and earnings tax-deferred until withdrawal.

Distributions taxed as ordinary income.
​

Roth contributions:

Made with after-tax dollars (no current tax deduction).

Qualified distributions tax-free (age 59½ and 5-year holding period met).

Contributions can be withdrawn tax-free at any time; earnings withdrawn before age 59½ subject to tax and penalty.
​

After-tax (non-Roth) contributions:

Contributions made with after-tax dollars.

Principal withdrawn tax-free.

Earnings taxed as ordinary income upon withdrawal.
​

Employer contributions:

Not taxable when contributed.

Taxed as ordinary income upon distribution.
​

10.2 Early Withdrawal Penalties
Distributions taken before age 59½ are generally subject to a 10% early withdrawal penalty in addition to ordinary income tax.
​

Penalty exceptions may apply in cases such as:

Separation from service after age 55

Substantially equal periodic payments (SEPP)

Disability

Medical expenses exceeding a certain percentage of adjusted gross income

Qualified domestic relations order (QDRO)

IRS levy

Certain birth or adoption expenses (up to $5,000)
​

10.3 ERISA Protections
The Plan is governed by the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA), which provides important protections:
​

Fiduciary standards for plan management

Participant rights to plan information

Protection against creditors (assets generally cannot be seized)

Appeals process for denied claims or benefits

Disclosure requirements
​
