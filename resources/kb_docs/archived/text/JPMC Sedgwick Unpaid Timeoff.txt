Policy Document for U.S. Employees
Short-Term Disability (STD) | Long-Term Disability (LTD) | Leave Administration
Effective Date: January 1, 2025
​

JPMorgan Chase partners with Sedgwick Claims Management Services to administer disability benefits and leave of absence programs for employees unable to work due to illness or injury. This document outlines Short-Term Disability (STD), Long-Term Disability (LTD), integrated leave administration via mySedgwick, medical certification and claims processes, return-to-work support, and coordination with state disability programs.
​

Sedgwick is a third-party administrator (TPA) that manages disability and leave claims on behalf of JPMorgan Chase, while the firm funds the benefit payments.
​

JPMorgan Chase Sedgwick Disability Management – Executive Summary
Sedgwick responsibilities include:
​

Receiving and processing STD and LTD claims

Reviewing medical documentation

Determining eligibility

Calculating benefits and payment schedules

Coordinating state disability benefits

Managing return-to-work processes and leave tracking

Providing customer service, claim updates, and appeals handling

JPMorgan Chase responsibilities include:
​

Establishing plan design and eligibility rules

Maintaining employee data and service records

Coordinating job protection and accommodations

Overseeing Sedgwick’s claim administration

Ensuring legal compliance

Section 2: Short-Term Disability (STD) Plan
2.1 STD Plan Overview
The STD Plan provides time off and disability pay for non-occupational illness or injury.
​

Key features:

Up to 26 weeks of job-protected disability leave

Full or partial pay based on years of service

14-calendar-day elimination (waiting) period

Covers non-work-related illness and injury

Coordinates with state disability benefits

No employee premium (employer-paid)

Administered by Sedgwick
​

2.2 STD Eligibility Requirements
Employees must:
​

Be actively employed on a U.S. payroll

Be benefits-eligible (20+ hours/week)

Be unable to perform material duties of their job due to illness/injury

Be under active care of a licensed provider

Not be disabled due to work-related injury/illness (Workers’ Comp)

No waiting period for coverage:

STD eligibility begins as soon as an employee becomes benefits-eligible; no minimum service requirement.
​

Part-time employees (20+ hours/week) are eligible on the same basis as full-time employees.
​

2.3 Qualifying Disabilities
Covered conditions (non-occupational) include:
​

Serious illness requiring hospitalization or ongoing treatment

Major surgery and recovery

Pregnancy and childbirth (medical recovery period)

Serious injury (fractures, severe sprains)

Mental health conditions requiring time off

Chronic conditions with acute flare-ups

Cancer treatment and recovery

Cardiovascular events and treatment

Complications from procedures

Excluded conditions:

Work-related injuries/illnesses (Workers’ Comp)

Disabilities from commission of a felony

Self-inflicted injuries

Disabilities while incarcerated

Cosmetic procedures unless medically necessary

Disabilities occurring while not actively employed
​

2.4 Elimination Period
14-calendar-day waiting period:

Employee must be continuously disabled for 14 consecutive days before STD benefits begin.
​

Using sick time:

Employees may use accrued sick time, vacation, or personal days during the elimination period; otherwise this time is unpaid.
​

Elimination period calculation example:
​

Day 1: First day unable to work (e.g., Mar 1)

Days 1–14: Elimination period

Day 15: STD benefits start if claim approved

2.5 STD Benefit Amounts and Duration
Benefit schedule (up to 26 weeks total):
​

Less than 1 year service: 0 weeks at 100% pay; up to 26 weeks at 60% pay

1–<2 years: 5 weeks at 100%; up to 21 weeks at 60%

2–<5 years: 10 weeks at 100%; up to 16 weeks at 60%

5–<10 years: 15 weeks at 100%; up to 11 weeks at 60%

10+ years: 26 weeks at 100%; 0 weeks at 60%

All employees may receive up to 26 weeks of STD; the mix of full vs partial pay depends on tenure.
​

Base salary:

STD is calculated on base salary only (no bonuses, overtime, commissions), using the salary at disability start.
​

Payments:

Paid biweekly through payroll starting after the 14-day elimination period, subject to applicable taxes.
​

2.6 State Disability Benefit Coordination
States with statutory disability programs include: California, New York, New Jersey, Rhode Island, Hawaii.
​

California and Rhode Island:
​

JPMC STD benefits are offset by state disability benefits.

Total benefits cannot exceed 100% of salary during full-pay periods, or 60% in partial-pay periods.

State benefits are deducted from JPMC STD even if the employee does not apply.

Other states (NY, NJ, HI):

Employee receives the higher of state benefit or JPMC STD; state programs and STD are coordinated for the most generous outcome.
​

Employee responsibilities:

Apply for state disability, provide documentation, coordinate with Sedgwick, and report state benefit amounts.
​

2.7 Filing an STD Claim
When to file:

As soon as it is known that the employee will be unable to work more than 14 consecutive days.
​

How to file:
​

Notify manager of inability to work and expected duration.

Log in to mySedgwick (login.mysedgwick.com/jpmc).

Start a new claim (“Report a New Claim” / “Start a Claim”).

Complete claim form with personal and disability details and expected return date.

Authorize Sedgwick to obtain medical records.

Submit the claim.

Claim filing deadline:

File within 30 days of disability start; late claims may be denied or delayed.
​

Alternative filing:

Phone (Sedgwick Leave Service Center) or manager assistance, if needed.
​

2.8 Medical Certification Requirements
Sedgwick requires a physician statement documenting:
​

Diagnosis and condition

Disability start date

Symptoms and functional limitations

Treatment plan and prognosis

Expected duration of disability

Inability to perform job duties

Expected return-to-work date

Sedgwick provides forms; the physician should return them directly to Sedgwick.
​

Documentation deadline:

Medical certification must be submitted within 30 days of claim filing; late documentation can lead to denial.
​

Ongoing updates:

Additional medical updates may be required for claim extensions beyond the initial approval.
​

2.9 Claim Review and Approval Process
Sedgwick review steps:
​

Receive claim and medical documentation.

Medical review team evaluates information.

Verify that the disability meets the plan definition and the employee is eligible.

Issue approval or denial.

Decision timeline:

Typically 7–10 business days after Sedgwick receives complete medical documentation.
​

Claim approval:

Employee receives an approval letter with benefit amount and duration.

Payments start after the elimination period, via payroll.

A case manager is assigned.
​

Claim denial:

Employee receives written notice with reasons and appeal rights.
​

Employee responsibilities during STD:

Respond to Sedgwick, attend appointments, follow treatment, provide updates, and notify of changes or early return.
​

2.10 During STD Leave
Sedgwick monitors the claim and checks status periodically.
​

Benefits continuation during approved STD:
​

Medical, dental, vision coverage and life/disability insurance continue.

Employee premium share is deducted from STD payments.

Service credit accrues and job protection is maintained.

Leave coordination:

STD runs concurrently with FMLA (if eligible), state leave programs, and possibly personal leave.
​

2.11 Returning from STD Leave
Return-to-work process:
​

Obtain physician’s release.

Notify Sedgwick with return date.

Submit medical release.

Coordinate return date and schedule with manager.

Resume duties in the same position.

Fitness-for-duty:

Physician must confirm fitness to perform essential functions and note restrictions, if any.

Manager, HR, and Sedgwick coordinate accommodations and transitional return-to-work, such as reduced hours or modified duties.
​

Recurrent disabilities:

If the same condition recurs within 90 days: treated as a continuation of original disability, no new elimination period, same 26-week maximum.

If recurrence occurs after 90 days: treated as a new disability with a new elimination period and benefit period.
​

2.12 STD Claim Appeals
Employees have the right to appeal a denial or termination of STD benefits.
​

Appeal process:
​

Review denial letter and deadlines.

Gather additional medical evidence.

Submit written appeal via mySedgwick or mail.

Include all supporting documents.

Sedgwick reviews and issues decision in 30–45 days.

Appeals must be filed within 180 days of the denial notice.
​

Second-level appeal:

Available if the first-level appeal is denied; may include independent medical review.
​

Legal rights:

After exhausting appeals, employees may file a complaint with regulators or pursue ERISA-based legal action; legal counsel may assist.
​

2.13 Transition to Long-Term Disability
When STD benefits are exhausted (after 26 weeks), Sedgwick evaluates the employee for LTD eligibility, provided LTD coverage was in place before disability.
​

Sedgwick assists with LTD application and may request additional medical documentation.
​

Section 3: Long-Term Disability (LTD) Plan
3.1 LTD Plan Overview
The LTD Plan provides income replacement for disabilities extending beyond 26 weeks.
​

Key features:

50–60% of Total Annual Cash Compensation (TACC) replacement

Benefits may continue to age 65 or until recovery

Two options: Group LTD and Individual Disability Insurance (IDI)

Monthly payments

SSDI offset applies

Administered by Sedgwick or an insurance carrier
​

3.2 LTD Eligibility Requirements
Employees must:
​

Be actively employed and benefits-eligible when disability began

Have LTD coverage (automatic Group LTD or elected IDI) in force before disability began

Be under the care of a licensed physician

Disability definition:

First 24 months: Unable to perform the material duties of own occupation.

After 24 months: Unable to perform duties of any occupation for which reasonably qualified by education, training, or experience.
​

3.3 LTD Coverage Options and Benefit Amounts
Coverage options:
​

Group LTD (automatic): For employees with TACC < $60,000; typically 60% of TACC.

IDI (optional): Higher benefits (up to ~60–66%), customizable coverage, portable if employee leaves, may include cost-of-living adjustments.

Employees with TACC ≥ $60,000 must elect LTD coverage (Group LTD or IDI) during annual enrollment to have LTD protection.
​

Maximum monthly benefit is capped (commonly $10,000–$15,000, depending on plan).
​

SSDI offset:

LTD benefits are reduced by Social Security Disability Insurance benefits.

Sedgwick assists with SSDI applications; employees are required to apply.
​

Example:
​

Calculated LTD: $5,000/month

SSDI: $2,000/month

LTD pays $3,000; total disability income remains $5,000.

3.4 LTD Benefit Duration
LTD ends at the earliest of:
​

Employee reaching age 65

Recovery or no longer meeting disability definition

Failure to provide required medical documentation

Death

For disabilities starting after age 60, benefit duration may be limited (e.g., 12 months to 5 years).
​

3.5 Filing and Administering an LTD Claim
Claim process:
​

Automatic evaluation near the end of STD.

Complete LTD application via mySedgwick.

Provide comprehensive medical records.

Apply for SSDI.

Claim is reviewed and approved or denied.

Medical documentation:

Detailed physician statements, test results, functional capacity evaluations, and specialist reports may be required.
​

Ongoing requirements:

Provide medical updates every 3–6 months.

Attend independent medical exams (IMEs) if requested.

Participate in vocational rehabilitation if offered.

Report any work income or return-to-work activity.
​

Benefits continuation during LTD:

Health insurance may continue for a period, with premiums paid by employee; after 24 months, coverage may transition to COBRA.

Life insurance may continue at reduced levels; service credit does not accrue; job is not held indefinitely.
​

Return-to-work programs:

Vocational rehab, trial work periods, and partial disability benefits may be available; HR coordinates accommodations.
​

Appeals:

LTD appeals follow a process similar to STD, with internal appeals and potential legal remedies under ERISA.
​

Section 5: Employee Rights and Protections
5.1 Job Protection
Job protection:

Maintained during approved STD (up to 26 weeks) and under FMLA if eligible; some state laws may extend protection.
​

LTD:

Employment relationship may continue, but the specific job is not held indefinitely after extended LTD; status should be discussed with HR.
​

5.2 Privacy and Confidentiality
Medical information:

Kept confidential by Sedgwick, protected under HIPAA.

Medical details are not shared with supervisors, only fitness-for-duty status.

Medical files are separate from personnel files.
​

Employee rights:

Access own medical records with Sedgwick and request corrections, limit disclosures, and file privacy complaints.
​

5.3 Non-Retaliation
Employees cannot be penalized for:
​

Filing disability claims

Taking approved disability or leave time

Adverse actions based on disability status or leave usage are prohibited and can be reported to HR or authorities.

JPMorgan Chase may amend, modify, or terminate disability plans at any time, subject to applicable law.

Disability benefits coordinate with:

Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)

State disability benefits

Other disability income sources

7.5 Tax Implications
STD:

Employer-paid, generally taxable income, reported on W-2 and subject to withholding.
​

LTD:

Group LTD (employer-paid) benefits are taxable.

IDI (employee-paid) benefits may be tax-free, subject to individual tax circumstances.
​

Conclusion
The Sedgwick-administered STD and LTD programs provide up to 26 weeks of job-protected short-term disability coverage and longer-term income protection for extended disabilities, integrated with FMLA and state programs via the mySedgwick portal. Employees should file claims promptly, maintain communication with Sedgwick case managers, and use available HR and EAP resources for additional support.
