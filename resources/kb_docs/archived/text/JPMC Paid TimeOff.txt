Policy Document for U.S. Employees
Parental Leave | Critical Caregiver Leave | Sick Time | Bereavement Leave
Effective Date: January 1, 2025
​

JPMorgan Chase provides comprehensive paid leave benefits to support employees during significant life events and personal challenges. This document outlines paid leave programs effective January 1, 2025, including Parental Leave (16 weeks for all new parents), Critical Caregiver Leave (up to 4 weeks), Sick Time (up to 10 days annually), and Bereavement Leave (up to 20 days for immediate family loss).
​

These policies reflect the firm’s commitment to work-life balance, family support, and employee well-being.
​

JPMorgan Chase Paid Leave Policies – Executive Summary
JPMorgan Chase provides 16 weeks of fully paid parental leave to all eligible employees for the birth or placement of a child for adoption or surrogacy. The policy is gender-neutral and applies equally to all parents regardless of gender, marital status, or caregiver role.
​

Section 1: Parental Leave Policy
1.1 Policy Overview
Key features:
​

16 continuous workweeks of paid leave for all parents

Available for birth, adoption, or surrogacy placement

Gender-neutral, no “primary” vs “secondary” caregiver distinction

12-month window to use leave following birth or placement

Full salary continuation during leave

Job-protected leave under applicable law

1.2 Eligibility Requirements
To qualify for Parental Leave, employees must:
​

Be actively employed by JPMorgan Chase on a U.S. payroll

Be regularly scheduled to work 20 or more hours per week

Have benefits-eligible status (not temporary, seasonal, or contingent)

Experience a qualifying event (birth, adoption, surrogacy placement, or foster-to-adopt)

New hires are eligible once they meet benefits eligibility.
​

1.3 Leave Duration and Timing
Qualifying events:
​

Birth of an employee’s biological child

Adoption placement

Surrogacy placement

Foster care placement with intent to adopt

Duration and timing:

Up to 16 continuous workweeks of paid leave (one workweek = five scheduled workdays)

Part-time employees receive a proportionate amount based on scheduled hours

Leave may begin any time within 12 months of birth or placement

Leave must be taken continuously, not intermittently
​

12-month window:

Employees have 12 months from the date of birth or placement to complete the 16 weeks.
​

Multiple parents:

Each parent employed by JPMorgan Chase is independently eligible for the full 16 weeks.

Leave may be taken concurrently or consecutively, providing up to 32 weeks collectively.
​

Multiple births/placements:

Twins, triplets, or multiple adoptions count as a single event (still 16 weeks total).
​

1.4 Parental Leave for Multiple Parents
Each JPMC-employed parent receives 16 weeks for the same qualifying event.

Timing can differ for each parent within the 12-month window.
​

1.5 Compensation During Parental Leave
Salary continuation:

Employees receive 100% of base salary during Parental Leave.

Regular payroll schedule continues.
​

Benefits continuation:

Medical, dental, and vision coverage

Life and disability insurance

401(k) matching and automatic pay credits (subject to compensation)

Benefit accruals and service credit

Employee perks and discounts
​

Parental Leave does not reduce other PTO balances (vacation, personal days).
​

1.6 Interaction with Other Leave Policies
FMLA:

For eligible employees, Parental Leave runs concurrently with FMLA.

First 12 weeks satisfy FMLA requirements; remaining 4 weeks are protected by JPMC policy.
​

State paid family leave:

State benefits (e.g., CA, NJ, NY, WA) are coordinated so the employee receives no less than 100% of salary in total.

HR provides state-specific guidance.
​

Short-Term Disability (STD):

For childbirth, STD typically covers 6–8 weeks of medical recovery.

STD runs concurrently with Parental Leave; no stacking of pay beyond 100%.

After disability period, remaining Parental Leave continues.
​

1.7 Notice and Documentation Requirements
Notice:

Recommended: 30 days’ notice before anticipated leave start when possible.

For birth: As soon as practicable after learning of pregnancy.

For adoption/surrogacy: As soon as placement date is known.

Emergencies: Notice as soon as possible.
​

Documentation (may be required):
​

Birth certificate or hospital documentation

Adoption decree or placement papers

Surrogacy agreement or placement confirmation

1.8 Returning from Parental Leave
Return to work:

Employees return to the same or an equivalent position with the same pay, benefits, and terms.

Seniority and service credit continue to accrue.

No adverse action may be taken for using Parental Leave.
​

Failure to return:

Employment may be terminated.

In some circumstances, the firm may seek repayment of benefits paid during leave; health coverage may transition to COBRA.
​

Extension:

Employees who need additional time beyond 16 weeks may request unpaid Personal Leave (up to 26 weeks), additional FMLA (if available), or state leave, in consultation with HR.
​

1.9 Additional Parental Support Programs
parents@jpmc program:

Resources, webinars, and guidance on balancing work and family.
​

SNOO infant smart sleeper bassinet:

Free five-month rental for newborn-eligible dependents with a $99 refundable deposit.
​

Lactation support:

24/7 access to board-certified lactation consultants

On-site lactation rooms at many locations

Free breastfeeding kit and materials

Milk shipping services during business travel

Discounts on pumps and supplies
​

Maternity support:

Health assessments, nurse support during pregnancy via medical plans.
​

Family building assistance:

Fertility medical services (lifetime max $35,000)

Fertility prescription coverage (lifetime max $15,000)

Adoption and surrogacy benefits (combined lifetime max $50,000)
​

Child care and tutoring:

Backup child care via Bright Horizons

JPMC-dedicated child care centers

Enhanced family support sourcing caregivers

College Coach education planning
​

1.10 Policy History and Legal Context
Pre-2023:

16 weeks for “primary caregivers,” 6 weeks for “non-primary caregivers.”
​

2023 enhancement:

16 weeks for all parents, regardless of caregiver role.
​

Legal background:

2019 class-action settlement (~$5 million) over alleged discrimination in parental leave for fathers.

Settlement required gender-neutral administration, training, and removal of “primary/secondary” distinctions.
​

Current policy:

Complies with Title VII, exceeds FMLA, and aligns with industry-leading practices.
​

Section 2: Critical Caregiver Leave Policy
2.1 Policy Overview
Critical Caregiver Leave provides up to 4 continuous workweeks of paid leave to care for a seriously ill or injured family member.
​

Key features:
​

Up to 4 continuous workweeks (20 workdays for full-time)

Full salary continuation

For serious health events affecting immediate family members

Leave must be continuous (no intermittent use)

Job-protected

2.2 Eligibility Requirements
Employees must:
​

Be on a U.S. payroll

Work at least 20 hours per week

Have benefits-eligible status

Need to provide care for an eligible family member with a serious health condition

Eligible family members:

Spouse or domestic partner

Child (biological, adopted, step, foster, or legal ward)

Parent (biological, adoptive, step, or legal guardian)
​

2.3 Qualifying Serious Health Conditions
Examples:
​

Major surgery or procedure

Hospitalization

Serious illness requiring treatment and recovery

Significant injury needing care and assistance

Chronic or terminal illness requiring intensive caregiving

Mental health crisis requiring immediate support

Medical certification may be required, including:
​

Nature of condition

Expected duration of care

Confirmation employee’s presence is needed

2.4 Leave Duration and Usage
Up to 4 continuous workweeks (pro-rated for part-time).

Must be taken all at once; cannot be broken into separate periods.

Cannot be extended by combining with other paid leaves.
​

The policy does not specify a strict annual event limit, but HR should be consulted for multiple events in a year.
​

2.5 Compensation During Critical Caregiver Leave
100% base salary; regular payroll schedule.

Does not draw down vacation, personal days, or sick time.

Medical, dental, vision, life, disability, 401(k) match, and accruals continue.
​

2.6 Interaction with Other Leave Policies
FMLA:

Runs concurrently with FMLA when caring for a spouse, child, or parent with a serious health condition (or military caregiver leave).

Critical Caregiver Leave satisfies the FMLA entitlement during the 4 weeks; additional FMLA, if available, is unpaid.
​

State paid family leave:

State benefits coordinate so the employee receives no less than 100% of salary in total.
​

Personal Leave:

If more time is needed beyond 4 weeks, employees may request unpaid Personal Leave (up to 26 weeks) and rely on remaining FMLA if available.
​

2.7 Notice and Documentation Requirements
Notice:

For planned procedures: 30 days’ advance notice recommended.

Emergencies: Notify supervisor/HR as soon as possible.
​

Documentation:

Medical certification documenting serious health condition, duration, and need for care, with provider signature.

Typically due within ~15 days of request.
​

2.8 Returning from Critical Caregiver Leave
Return:

Employees return to the same or equivalent position with no adverse action.
​

If further time is needed:

Employee may request additional unpaid Personal Leave or FMLA, with updated certification and HR discussion.
​

2.9 Additional Caregiver Support Resources
LifeCare services:

24/7 support for elder care, in-home assessments, care plans, and referrals (877-576-2007).
​

EAP:

Confidential counseling, up to 8 free therapy sessions per year, caregiver stress resources, 24/7 crisis support.
​

Health Advocate:

Navigation help for complex healthcare issues, provider search, appointment scheduling, and claims support.
​

Section 3: Sick Time Policy
3.1 Policy Overview
Sick time supports employee and family health, appointments, and recovery.
​

Key features:
​

Full-time: Up to 10 days (80 hours) per year

Part-time: Accrued sick time (max 96 hours)

No carryover (subject to local law); no payout at termination

Available for employee illness and certain family care

3.2 Eligibility and Allocation
Full-time employees (30+ hours/week):
​

Receive 10 sick days (80 hours) each January 1.

New hires receive prorated amounts based on hire date:

Jan 1–Mar 31: 10 days

Apr 1–Jun 30: 7.5 days

Jul 1–Sep 30: 5 days

Oct 1–Dec 31: 2.5 days

Part-time employees (20–29 hours/week):

Earn sick time via accrual based on hours worked, capped at 96 hours.

Accrual rates vary by state/local law.
​

3.3 Permitted Uses of Sick Time
Employee’s own health:

Illness or injury

Medical, dental, or vision appointments

Mental health visits

Preventive care and routine checkups

Recovery from procedures

Pregnancy-related medical conditions
​

Family care:

Spouse or domestic partner

Children

Parents/legal guardians

Other family as required by local law
​

Other uses:

Medical care, legal proceedings, and related time off for domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking, as required by law.
​

3.4 Notification Requirements
Notify supervisor as soon as practicable; for planned appointments, give advance notice.

For unexpected illness, notify before start time or as reasonably possible.
​

Documentation:

JPMC may require documentation for absences beyond 3 consecutive days (e.g., provider note).

Requirements aligned with applicable laws.
​

3.5 Sick Time Administration
Minimum usage increment is typically 1 hour.

Sick time is recorded via timekeeping systems.

Unused sick time does not carry over, except where local law requires (e.g., CA, NYC).

Unused sick time is not paid out at termination and has no cash value.
​

3.6 Interaction with Other Leave Policies
Sick time may cover the STD elimination period (e.g., first 14 days).

STD can then provide partial/full pay up to 26 weeks.

Sick time can run concurrently with FMLA when the condition is FMLA-qualifying.

Sick time is separate from Parental Leave and Critical Caregiver Leave; those leaves do not reduce sick balances.
​

3.7 Wellness and Preventive Care
JPMC encourages sick time use for:
​

Annual physicals and screenings

Immunizations, cancer screenings

Mental health check-ins and counseling

Many preventive services are covered at no cost in-network under medical plans.
​

Section 4: Bereavement Leave Policy
4.1 Policy Overview
Bereavement leave supports employees following the death of a family member.
​

Key features:
​

Immediate family: Up to 20 continuous workdays

Extended family: 5 consecutive workdays

Fully paid at 100% salary

Job-protected, with flexible and compassionate administration

4.2 Bereavement Leave Entitlements
Immediate family (20 days):

Spouse or domestic partner

Child (biological, adopted, step, foster, or legal ward)

Pregnancy loss (miscarriage or stillbirth)
​

Extended family (5 days):

Parent (including in-law)

Sibling (including in-law)

Grandparent (employee’s or spouse’s)

Grandchild

Other family as defined by policy
​

4.3 Timing and Usage
Immediate family:

Up to 20 continuous workdays, usually starting on or shortly after the date of death.

Flexibility for funeral/memorial timing.
​

Extended family:

5 consecutive workdays, generally around the time of services or arrangements.
​

Leave must be used within a reasonable timeframe; cultural and religious observances are accommodated when possible.
​

4.4 Compensation During Bereavement Leave
100% base salary with regular payroll.

Does not reduce vacation, personal, or sick time.

Health and insurance benefits, service credit, and accruals continue.
​

4.5 Notification Requirements
Notify supervisor as soon as reasonably possible.

HR should be informed to code the leave.
​

Documentation (if requested, handled sensitively):

Death certificate

Obituary or funeral program

Other reasonable verification
​

4.6 Additional Time Off
If more time is needed:

Employee may use vacation/personal days.

May request unpaid Personal Leave (up to 26 weeks).

FMLA may apply if the employee develops a serious health condition (e.g., clinical depression) as a result of grief.
​

4.7 Support Resources
Support includes:
​

EAP (Spring Health): Up to 8 free counseling sessions, 24/7 crisis line (877-576-2007)

Spring Health mental health services for grief and complicated grief

LifeCare services for practical assistance with funeral/estate and referrals

Manager and HR support for flexible schedules and accommodations

4.8 Miscarriage and Stillbirth Support
Miscarriage at any stage and stillbirth at or after 20 weeks both qualify for 20 days of bereavement leave.

Available to both parents if both work at JPMC.
​

Additional support:

Medical care via health plan, STD for physical recovery, mental health support, lactation support.

Requests are handled with privacy and sensitivity; details are limited to necessary verification.
​

Section 5: Leave Administration and Compliance
5.1 Requesting Leave
General process:
​

Notify supervisor of need for leave.

Contact HR or the Benefits Service Center.

Submit leave request and any required documentation.

Receive approval confirmation.

Coordinate work transition.

Leave management system:

Requests are processed via JPMC’s leave system and third-party administrators; phone support is available through the Benefits Service Center (1-844-ASK-JPMC).
​

5.2 Manager Responsibilities
Managers are expected to:
​

Respond supportively and compassionately

Ensure confidentiality

Coordinate with HR

Support re-entry and transitions

Avoid any adverse actions based on use of leave

Non-retaliation:

Leave usage cannot negatively affect performance evaluations, promotions, pay, or job status.
​

5.3 State and Local Law Compliance
JPMC complies with relevant state/local laws (e.g., CA, NY, NJ, WA). When policies differ, the more generous provision applies.
​

HR and leave administrators provide jurisdiction-specific guidance.
​

5.4 Privacy and Confidentiality
Protected information:

Medical data, family/personal circumstances, pregnancy, fertility, mental health, and related details.
​

Protections:

Medical documentation stored separately and securely.

Access limited to authorized personnel.

Shared only on a need-to-know basis.

Complies with HIPAA and ADA confidentiality rules.
​

5.5 Fraud Prevention and Policy Abuse
Employee expectations:

Use leave only for legitimate purposes.

Provide accurate information and genuine documentation.
​

Consequences of misuse:

Denial of leave, recovery of benefits, disciplinary action up to termination, and possible legal action.
​

5.6 Return-to-Work Process
General steps:
​

Confirm return date with supervisor/HR.

Complete any required documentation.

Attend re-entry meetings if needed.

Resume same or equivalent role.

Support:

Gradual or flexible return may be offered where appropriate.

Reasonable accommodations (e.g., lactation breaks, modified duties) are available.

Benefits continue without interruption; seniority is preserved.
​

Section 6: Additional Time-Off Benefits
6.1 Other Paid Time Off
Holidays:

Generally 10 bank holidays per year (Federal Reserve calendar, excluding Columbus Day), including New Year’s Day, MLK Jr. Day, Presidents Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Day After Thanksgiving, and Christmas.
​

Vacation:

3–5 weeks per year based on service and pay grade, accruing throughout the year.

Eligible for use after 90 days of employment.
​

Personal days:

3 paid personal days per year for all employees except Managing Directors.

Usable for any personal reason, including cultural or religious observances.
​

Volunteer/employee engagement:

1 paid day per year for volunteer activities, tied to community involvement and GoodWorks programs.
​

Civic duty:

Paid time off for jury duty, subpoenaed testimony, and voting (if unable to vote outside work hours).
​

6.2 Unpaid Leave Options
Personal Leave:

Up to 26 weeks unpaid in a 12-month period; minimum 4 weeks job-protected.

For personal reasons not covered by other policies.
​

FMLA:

Up to 12 weeks unpaid job-protected leave for qualifying reasons; up to 26 weeks for military caregiver leave, where eligible.
​

Military Leave:

Paid and unpaid time off for military service, with reemployment rights under USERRA.
